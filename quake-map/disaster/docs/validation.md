# Validation and checks

Numbers here are produced by the scripts named in each section. Anything not yet validated is said so.

## Exposure
<a id="exposure"></a>

Generated by `python tools/check_exposure.py --area noida` (full output: [exposure_check_noida.md](exposure_check_noida.md)).
Area: Noida box 77.30-77.55 E, 28.42-28.65 N, 483,358 Overture buildings (release 2026-11-18.0), Google heights 2021-2023
(pyramid-level read, see below), GHS-POP R2023A epoch 2025.

**Storeys (MASTER_PLAN 1.1 / 1.4)**

| source | buildings | share |
|---|---|---|
| Google 2.5D height | 322,555 | 66.7% |
| mapped label (`num_floors` / height) | 1,025 | 0.2% |
| heuristic, Google height unusable (< 2 m or spread > 6 m) | 159,778 | 33.1% |

* Target "> 80% measured" is **not met in Noida (66.9%)**. Cause: 30% of footprints have a median height < 2 m in the product
  (62% of footprints under 20 m2, 47% of 20-50 m2): small low buildings are not resolved. Only 2.7% fail on spread. They keep
  the old heuristic and are flagged `guess_unstable`. (The smaller 6 x 5 km baseline box reaches 81.8%.)
* Agreement with the 994 Noida buildings that carry a `num_floors` label (762 with a usable Google height):
  exact 27%, within +/-1 storey **68.8% (target 70%, missed)**, bias -0.45 storeys at 2.8 m per storey.
* **Storey height = 2.8 m**: the value in 2.8-3.5 m with the smallest absolute bias (scan in the generated file).
  The residual bias is not a storey-height effect: the median Google height of labelled buildings is 8.5 m (1-2 floors), 10.2 m
  (3-4), 12.3 m (5-6), 22.7 m (7+), so the product compresses heights (low-rise read high, tall read low). With 2.8 m the 3-4
  floor group is unbiased (-0.03), 1-2 floors +1.4, 5-6 floors -0.7, 7+ floors -1.5.
* The labelled buildings lean to tall apartment blocks (median 5 floors), so these figures describe that subset, not the
  unlabelled majority. No imagery spot-check of unlabelled buildings has been done.

**Height sampling approximation (measured, 6 x 5 km box, 2023, vs native 0.5 m pixels)**

| method | read time | mean abs diff | storeys identical | within +/-1 storey | bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| native 0.5 m | 101 s | - | - | - | - |
| 2 m nearest (`--method nearest`, default) | 76-99 s | 0.18 m | 95.1% | 100% | -0.055 m |
| 2 m average | 106 s | 0.19 m | 93.1% | 100% | -0.023 m |
| 2 m pyramid level (`--method overview`) | 6 s | 0.19 m | 93.1% | 100% | -0.023 m |

The Noida-wide run used `--method overview` for speed; the baseline box uses `nearest`.

**Population (1.3)**: night total 4,799,029 vs GHS-POP 4,905,499 for the area (-2.2%; target +/-10%). v1's typical-occupancy
method gave 8,514,503 (1.74x GHS-POP; 1.9x for the whole box in the plan). 118 of 681 one-kilometre cells used the neighbourhood
median factor (factor range 0.05-3). GHS-POP E2025 is a modelled estimate, not a census count.
Occupancy shares by time of day (`priors/occupancy.csv`) are approximate Hazus-style values and are **not verified** against the
Hazus 6.1 manual (FEMA's server refused automated download).

**Construction type**: the default census shares are an assumption (priors/census_materials.csv); no census table was
looked up. Type probabilities are therefore priors with a documented rule, not measurements.

## Replaying past earthquakes (MASTER_PLAN 3.6)
Generated by `python tools/validate_events.py --event <turkiye2023|nepal2015>`; full tables in
[validation_turkiye2023.md](validation_turkiye2023.md) and [validation_nepal2015.md](validation_nepal2015.md). Satellite grading
from Copernicus EMS (EMSR648, EMSR125), shaking from the USGS ShakeMaps, buildings from Overture, damage curves are the
uncalibrated placeholders. Skill = AUC for ranking damaged above undamaged buildings (0.5 = none), with cluster-bootstrap
intervals. The nodal plane that follows the USGS rupture polygon is used for our own shaking model.

**Honest summary: the evidence is weak.**
* **Kathmandu 2015 (626 graded buildings, 160 damaged): no skill shown.** AUC 0.43 with the ShakeMap's own shaking and 0.50 with ours;
  ranking by PGA alone is no better (0.43 / 0.52); the 500 m cell correlation is negative. The grading sample is not random (it
  follows where mapping teams looked) and damage in the valley depends on building vulnerability (old masonry, storeys) far more
  than on PGA, which our guessed storeys/types cannot capture. Google heights were not fetched for these areas.
* **Gaziantep 2023 (10,775 graded, only 7 damaged): AUC 0.86 (ShakeMap) / 0.75 (our shaking)**, intervals 0.80-0.96 / 0.43-0.90.
  Exposure adds a little over PGA alone (0.80 / 0.72). Seven positives is far too few for a firm claim.
* **Elbistan (9 damaged of 155)**: 0.70 / 0.60 with PGA alone matching or beating the full model. **Kirikhan** has no undamaged
  graded buildings, so only severity among damaged could be scored: destroyed vs damaged AUC 0.58 (ShakeMap) / 0.50 (ours).
* **Our shaking model vs the ShakeMap** (median ln ratio): Gaziantep -0.11, Elbistan +0.21, Kathmandu -0.46, Kirikhan -1.04
  (a factor of ~2.8 low, near the fault). A rectangular Wells & Coppersmith rupture is shorter than the real ~300 km rupture, which
  is the likely cause of the largest gap.
* Absolute counts are deliberately not compared (grading = visible damage, not engineering damage states).
* Not done: held-out Nepal districts (no survey data), Kathmandu/Turkiye replays with calibrated curves.
